Why I built this

I built Upright because of my own neck posture problems. Hours of screen time, whether for schoolwork or just scrolling, left me dealing with stiffness and discomfort I didn't fully understand — and I had no easy way to check whether what I was feeling actually matched bad posture, or to know what to do about it besides a vague sense that I should "sit up straight."

The problem

"Tech neck" — the strain that builds up from looking down at phones and leaning toward screens for hours a day — has become common enough that physical therapists and spine specialists now treat it as its own condition. As the head tilts forward, the effective load on the neck doesn't just increase a little: it can climb from around 10 pounds to as much as 60 pounds, depending on how far forward the head sits. Most people have no simple way to see this happening to their own posture in real time, let alone fix it.

Our approach

Upright uses your device's camera and on-device pose detection to measure how your head, neck, and shoulders are actually positioned — nothing is uploaded or stored, it all runs locally in your browser. It scores your posture out of 10 and matches exercises to the specific pattern it detects, instead of handing out one-size-fits-all advice. You can calibrate it to your own neutral posture for a more personal, accurate read.

Who it's for

Upright is for anyone who wants an easy, immediate way to check their own posture before deciding whether it's worth seeing a doctor or physical therapist — students doing hours of schoolwork, gamers, remote workers, or just anyone who's noticed their neck bothering them and wants a first, honest look at why.

What's next

With more time, I'd like to add posture tracking over days or weeks instead of just a single snapshot, gentle reminders during long screen sessions, and a way to export your posture history to share with a doctor or physical therapist.
